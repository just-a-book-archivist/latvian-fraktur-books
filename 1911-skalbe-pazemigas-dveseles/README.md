## Notes on the print

Two kinds of damaged type are easy to misread; both are kept as what the printer meant:

- A worn **`k`** whose small stroke at the stem did not print looks like **`l`** (`kà` looking
  like `là`, p. 32 and 41).
- A **circumflex** whose left stroke did not print looks like a **grave**: `brihdî` on p. 33
  looks like `brihdì`. A real grave sits further towards the middle of the letter.

The places where the reading was in doubt are noted in `transcription/anmerkungen.tsv`.

## Layout of the files

The general rules are in [`../EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES.md`](../EDITORIAL_GUIDELINES.md). In this
book:

- **Paragraphs** begin with four spaces, after a blank line (not at the top of a page).
- **Verse** within the prose (p. 41): one line of verse per line, eight spaces indent, a blank
  line before and after.
- **Section break:** the three asterisks of the print (two above, one below in the middle) are
  written as two lines, `*   *` and `  *`, with a blank line before and after.
- **Half-titles** (pp. 3, 21, 29, 43) as printed, line by line.

## The scans

`pages.tsv` gives the image for every printed page. The Internet Archive's set
(`pazemigasdwehsel00skaluoft_jp2.zip`) contains four extra camera shots of the open book (images
8, 9, 42, 43), so page *p* is image *p* + 6 for p. 1, *p* + 8 for pp. 2–33 and *p* + 10 from
p. 34.

In that scan the text of pp. 19, 24 and 25 is cut off at the inner margin. These three pages are
read from the copy of the National Library of Latvia (shelf mark LA/4297,
<https://gramatas.lndb.lv/viewerOpener?id=676815>), same setting, same line breaks; `pages.tsv`
links the images.
